## Sensitivity to λ

How much the top 10 depends on the shrinkage strength. `overlap` is the share of the λ = 2 top 10 that
is still top 10.

| λ | ρ vs λ=2 | top-10 overlap |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.25 | 0.953 | 90% |
| 0.5 | 0.981 | 100% |
| 1 | 0.996 | 100% |
| 2 | 1.000 | 100% |
| 5 | 0.993 | 100% |
| 10 | 0.989 | 90% |
| 50 | 0.976 | 90% |

As λ grows the model approaches the raw mean (no bias correction). Small λ trusts every judge's
estimated bias fully, including one-review judges. The ranking is stable across a wide middle band.

## Proof by simulation (40 random events)

Each simulated event has 40 projects with a hidden true quality ~ N(3, 0.6), 24 judges with a hidden
bias ~ N(0, 0.7) and noise ~ N(0, 0.35), 6 tracks, and 3 track-limited reviews per project, with
scores clamped to 1–5 like a real rubric. We ask how well each method recovers the *true* ranking.

| Method | mean ρ vs truth | worst seed | beats raw |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Raw mean | 0.802 | 0.599 | - |
| Shrunk z-score | 0.878 | 0.790 | 40/40 |
| Judge-bias model | 0.883 | 0.703 | 40/40 |
| Within-judge Bradley-Terry | 0.859 | 0.754 | 35/40 |

The test suite asserts the same property on 20 seeds (tests/test_normalization.py).
